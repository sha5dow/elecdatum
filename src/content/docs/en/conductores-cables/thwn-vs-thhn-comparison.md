## Thermal properties

| Characteristic | THHN | THWN-2 (dual) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Insulation material | PVC with nylon jacket | PVC with nylon jacket |
| Maximum temperature in dry | 90 °C / 194 °F | 90 °C / 194 °F |
| Maximum temperature in wet environment | Not specified (not suitable for wet locations) | 90 °C / 194 °F |

Classic THHN cable is limited to 90 °C in dry and has no rating for wet environments. The THWN (or THWN-2) designation adds the ability to operate in the presence of moisture. The THWN-2 version withstands 90 °C in both dry and wet, so most modern commercial products are supplied as dual THHN/THWN-2.

## Resistance to moisture and chemical agents

- **THHN**: Resistant to gasoline and oil, but not rated for installation in conduits that may accumulate water or in outdoor areas exposed to rain.
- **THWN/THWN-2**: Resistant to gasoline, oil, moisture, and water. Suitable for use in outdoor conduit, direct burial (when specified), and wet locations per NEC.

The nylon coating over PVC provides additional mechanical and chemical protection in both variants.

## Typical applications

| Application | THHN | THWN/THWN-2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Building wiring in dry conduit | Yes | Yes |
| Interior feeders (dry locations) | Yes | Yes |
| Installation outdoors or in wet locations | No | Yes |
| Connections for pumps, irrigation systems | No | Yes (with adequate protection) |
| Underground service entrances | No | Yes (THWN-2) |

Both types are used in control, lighting, and power circuits up to 600 V. The choice depends on the presence of moisture at the site.

## Key differences

| Aspect | THHN | THWN/THWN-2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meaning of the acronym | Thermoplastic High Heat-resistant Nylon-coated | Thermoplastic Heat and Water-resistant Nylon-coated |
| Maximum temperature in wet | Not specified | 75 °C (THWN) or 90 °C (THWN-2) |
| Water resistance | No | Yes |
| Outdoor use | Not permitted | Permitted |
| UL reference standard | UL 83 | UL 83 / UL 1063 (THWN-2) |
| Duality with THHN | – | Generally manufactured as THHN/THWN-2, combining both characteristics |

In practice, cables marked as THHN/THWN-2 cover all common needs, so pure THHN is rare in new installations.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### What is the maximum ampacity of a THWN cable 10 AWG in conduit?
A THWN copper conductor 10 AWG can carry up to 52 A under ambient temperature conditions of 30 °C, although correction factors for grouping and temperature are applied in actual installations.

### At what temperature can the insulation of a THWN-2 cable operate in a wet environment without degrading?
The insulation of THWN-2 is designed to withstand 90 °C continuously in a wet environment, equivalent to 194 °F.

### What is the electrical resistance of 1000 feet of THHN copper conductor 12 AWG?
A 12 AWG copper conductor has a resistance of approximately 1,6 Ω per 1000 feet (5,2 Ω/km), measured at 20 °C.

### How much voltage can a THWN-2 cable withstand between phase and ground?
The maximum rated voltage is 600 V, for both THHN and THWN-2, according to UL 83 and NEC standards.

### What gauge of THHN is needed to carry 200 A in a single-phase main feeder at 75 °C?
A 2/0 AWG copper conductor is required, which at 75 °C supports 225 A, although the exact ampacity depends on the insulation type and ambient temperature.

### What is the approximate outer diameter of a THHN/THWN-2 cable 4 AWG with insulation?
The total diameter is around 8–9 mm (0,31–0,35 in), adding the copper conductor (5,19 mm / 0.204 in) plus the thickness of PVC and the nylon jacket.

Type SJ, SJT and SO flexible cords are multi-conductor cables designed for portable or temporary electrical connections that require high flexibility. Their construction with extra-flexible copper conductors and resistant jackets makes them suitable for hard and extra-hard use conditions. The SJ designations indicate junior hard service with a nominal voltage of 300 V, while the letter T denotes thermoplastic insulation and jacket, and the O indicates oil resistance. S cords correspond to extra-hard service with a 600 V rating.

## Service, voltage and temperature
The nominal voltage for SJ and SJT series cords is 300 V, while type S and SO cords reach 600 V, allowing their use for powering higher-power industrial equipment. Versions with a thermoplastic PVC jacket (SJT) normally operate between −20 °C and 60 °C (−4 °F and 140 °F). Cords with rubber or neoprene insulation and jacket (SJ, SO) maintain flexibility over a wider range, typically from −40 °C to 90 °C (−40 °F to 194 °F), depending on the specific compound.

## Construction
Each flexible cord is composed of two or more stranded soft copper conductors class K or similar, with a cross-section ranging from 0,82 mm² (18 AWG) up to 33,6 mm² (2 AWG). The individual insulation can be PVC (thermoplastic) in SJT types, or EPDM rubber in SJ and SO types. The outer jacket, likewise of PVC or neoprene, groups the conductors in a round shape and provides mechanical and chemical resistance. Conductors are identified by colors according to the corresponding code (black, white, green for three conductors, adding red or blue for four conductors).

## Applications
SJ cords are used in portable power tools, heavy appliances, and workshop extensions operating at 120/240 V. SJT variants are intended for indoor use with medium mechanical stress, such as industrial vacuum cleaners or mobile lighting fixtures. SO and SOOW cords cover 480 V or 600 V applications in submersible pumps, construction equipment, temporary outdoor lighting systems, and industrial machinery where resistance to oil, moisture, and abrasion is required.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### What is the nominal voltage of a type SJ cord?
The type SJ cord has a nominal voltage of 300 V, which classifies it as junior hard service, and it can be used in 120 V or 240 V circuits.

### Up to what ambient temperature can a standard SJT cord operate?
An SJT cord with a PVC jacket operates continuously between −20 °C / −4 °F and 60 °C / 140 °F; at higher temperatures an ampacity correction factor must be applied.

### What sizes does the SO series cover?
SO cords cover from 18 AWG (0,82 mm²) up to 2 AWG (33,6 mm²), with two or more conductors, allowing management of loads up to 95 A in 2- to 3-conductor configurations.

### What is the current capacity of a 16 AWG three-conductor SJ cable?
An SJ 16 AWG cord with three conductors has a base ampacity of 10 A at 30 °C / 86 °F, according to reference values for copper cables with PVC insulation.

### What does the letter O mean in the SO or SJTO designation?
The letter O indicates that the outer jacket is oil-resistant; if a double O appears, for example in SOOW, both the insulation and the jacket are oil-resistant.

### How is the ampacity of an SJT cord derated when installed at 40 °C?
At 40 °C / 104 °F, the correction factor is 0,82; therefore, an SJT 14 AWG cord rated at 15 A is reduced to approximately 12,3 A.

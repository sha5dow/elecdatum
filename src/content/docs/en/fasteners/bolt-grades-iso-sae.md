The ISO (metric) and SAE (imperial) systems are the two dominant standards for classifying the mechanical strength of bolts and screws. This comparison brings together approximate equivalences, mechanical properties, allowable loads, and recommended tightening torques to facilitate selection between both systems in engineering applications.

## Main features

The ISO designation is defined by standard ISO 898‑1 and uses a property class (e.g., 8.8, 10.9). The first number multiplied by 100 gives the minimum tensile strength in MPa; the product of both numbers multiplied by 10 gives the approximate minimum yield strength. SAE grades (J429) are identified by a number (Grade 2, Grade 5, Grade 8) and are based on imperial units (psi). The following table shows the generally accepted equivalences in industry: an 8.8 ≈ Grade 5, a 10.9 ≈ Grade 8, and a 12.9 exceeds any common SAE grade.

## Equivalences between standards

| 4.6 | – | DIN 931 / 933 | Low/medium carbon steel | Light loads, non-critical structures |
| 4.8 | Grade 2 | DIN 931 / 933 | Low/medium carbon steel | Low-demand general fastening |
| 5.8 | Grade 2 | DIN 931 / 933 | Medium carbon steel | General use in machinery |

| 10.9 | Grade 8 | DIN 931 / 933 / 912 | Alloy steel, quenched and tempered | High-strength connections |

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between an 8.8 bolt and an SAE Grade 5?
Although an 8.8 has a minimum tensile strength of 830 MPa / 120 350 psi and a Grade 5 of 827 MPa / 120 000 psi, they are practically interchangeable in most applications; the 8.8 has a slightly higher yield strength (600 MPa / 87 000 psi vs. 586 MPa / 85 000 psi).

### Can I replace a 10.9 bolt with a Grade 8 without modifying the torque?
Yes, because the 10.9 has a proof strength of 830 MPa / 120 400 psi and the Grade 8 of 827 MPa / 120 000 psi, very close values. However, it is recommended to apply the torque specified for the lower grade if in doubt.

### Does a 12.9 bolt have a direct SAE equivalent?
No. With 970 MPa / 140 700 psi proof and 1 220 MPa / 176 900 psi tensile, it exceeds Grade 8. It should only be used when the drawings expressly specify it due to its lower ductility.

### What tightening torque is required for a dry M12 8.8?
Under unlubricated assembly conditions and coefficient of friction 0.15, the recommended torque is 80 N·m / 59 ft·lb.

### What is the correct drill bit for tapping M10 x 1.5 with a tap?
The pre‑drill bit for 1.5 mm pitch is 8.5 mm / 0.335 in, guaranteeing approximately 75% thread height.

### What is the equivalence between class 4.6 and SAE grades?
Class 4.6 offers a tensile strength of 400 MPa / 58 000 psi, lower than Grade 2 (410 MPa / 60 000 psi for small diameters), so there is no direct equivalent and it is typically used in joints without structural load.

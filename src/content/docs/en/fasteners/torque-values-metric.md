Control of tightening torque in metric threaded joints is essential to ensure proper preload without exceeding the yield strength of the bolt. The values in this guide are based on coarse ISO metric threads, with slightly lubricated threads and an assembly stress equivalent to 85 % of the proof load. Data is presented for the most commonly used property classes in engineering — 8.8, 9.8, 10.9 and 12.9 — according to ISO 898‑1 and the withdrawn ASTM F568M.

## Factors Affecting Tightening Torque

A 30% increase in torque is necessary when bolts are tightened dry, compared to the value for lightly lubricated threads. Conversely, generous lubrication can reduce the required torque by up to 40%. The tables in this document assume light oil lubrication.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the maximum torque for an M8 class 8.8 bolt?

28,8 Nm (21,2 lb‑ft) when the thread is lightly lubricated and the preload is limited to 85 % of the proof load.

### What tightening torque should be applied to an M12 class 10.9 bolt?

143 Nm (105,5 lb‑ft) for coarse metric thread with light lubrication, according to the recommended torque table.

### Is a 30% increase in torque sufficient to compensate for lack of lubrication?

Yes, when threads are assembled completely dry, approximately 30% more torque is required to achieve the same preload as with light lubrication, although it is recommended to verify by testing.

### What is the minimum tensile strength of a class 12.9 bolt?

1 220 MPa (176,9 ksi) for all diameters, according to ISO 898‑1.

### What drill bit is needed for an M10 × 1,5 threaded hole?

An 8,5 mm (0,335 in) diameter drill bit is required.

### Are metric class 8.8 bolts according to ASTM F568M and ISO 898‑1 equivalent?

Yes, the mechanical properties of class 8.8 defined in the old ASTM F568M coincide with those of ISO 898‑1; both standards are interchangeable at the design level.

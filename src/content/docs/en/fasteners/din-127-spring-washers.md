DIN 127 spring washers cover **18 standardized diameters** from M1.6 to M39, with section thicknesses ranging from 0.3 mm / 0.012 in to 7.0 mm / 0.276 in. Designed as open rings with rectangular section and offset ends, they generate an elastic force that prevents loosening in bolted joints subjected to vibration.

## Materials and finishes

The most common base material is **C67S spring steel** (1.1231), hardened and tempered to achieve a surface hardness of 42 to 50 HRC. For corrosive environments, **A2 stainless steel** (1.4301) or **A4** (1.4401) is used, with hardness in the range 250‑350 HV. Surface finishes include electrolytic zinc plating (Fe/Zn 8), zinc dichromate, nickel plating, and phosphating; in parentheses, stainless steel washers are supplied uncoated or with passivation.

## Mechanical properties

The elastic capacity of the DIN 127 washer is guaranteed by a **high-strength steel with minimum yield strength Rp0.2 ≥ 1250 MPa / 181 ksi**. The compression test according to DIN 267‑26 requires that after being compressed to 75% of its initial free height, the washer shows no appreciable permanent deformation. Typical hardness values are summarized in the following table.

| Material | Hardness HRC (HV) | Tensile strength Rm (MPa / ksi) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spring steel C67S | 42 – 50 HRC (412 – 509 HV) | 1300 – 1700 / 188 – 246 |
| Stainless steel A2 | 25 – 35 HRC (255 – 335 HV) | 700 – 900 / 101 – 130 |

## Standard equivalences

There is no direct ISO replacement for spring washers, since the **ISO 7089** series covers only flat washers. However, DIN 127 functionally overlaps with the following international standards:

| Standard | Description | Equivalence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DIN 127 B | Spring washer, flat ends (most widely used) | BN 672 (Bossard), functional equivalent to ASME B18.21.1 type B |
| ASME B18.21.1 | Helical spring lock washers (split lock washers) | Interchangeable in inches; for metric, DIN 127 is recommended |
| UNI 1751 | Spring washer (Italy) | Dimensions virtually identical to DIN 127 |
| BS 4464 | Spring lock washers (United Kingdom) | Metric equivalent; currently withdrawn and replaced by DIN 127 |
| JIS B 1251 | Spring lock washers (Japan) | Same dimensions for the covered diameters |

## Applications

DIN 127 washers are used in bolted joints subjected to **moderate dynamic loads and temperatures up to 200 °C / 392 °F in carbon steel**, although the A2 stainless steel version withstands up to 300 °C / 572 °F. They are common in agricultural machinery, steel structures, transportation equipment, and home appliances. They are not recommended for critical joints where precise preload control is required, nor for flanges with O-rings, where flat washers with surface treatment offer better sealing.

## Design notes

- Reusing a DIN 127 washer after disassembly **reduces its elastic capacity by at least 30%**, therefore systematic replacement is recommended.
- The washer must rest on a flat surface perpendicular to the bolt axis; on parts with cast surfaces, a flat washer DIN 125 should be interposed.
- The cut end is oriented toward the fixed piece and the raised end toward the bolt head or nut.
- For diameters larger than M39, wave spring washers or chemical locking systems are used.

## FAQ

**1. What are the dimensions of a DIN 127 washer for an M10 bolt?**  
The DIN 127 B washer for M10 has an inner diameter of 10.2 mm / 0.402 in, outer diameter of 18.1 mm / 0.713 in, and thickness of 2.5 mm / 0.098 in, with an approximate weight of 1.64 g / 0.058 oz per piece.

**2. What is the recommended tightening torque for an M8 8.8 bolt with a spring washer in dry condition?**  
The recommended dry tightening torque for M8 class 8.8 is 23 Nm / 16.96 ft·lb; if the bolt is lubricated, the torque is reduced to 17 Nm / 12.54 ft·lb to avoid overloading the washer.

**3. What is the maximum force to which an M12 class 8.8 can be tightened with a DIN 127 washer?**  
The maximum allowable clamping force for M12 8.8 is 37.9 kN / 8.53 kip, equivalent to 75% of the bolt proof load, which keeps the washer within the elastic regime.

**4. Which ANSI standard is equivalent to DIN 127?**  
The functional equivalent in inches is ASME B18.21.1 (helical spring lock washers), which defines two types: type A with beveled ends similar to DIN 127 A, and type B with flat ends analogous to DIN 127 B.

**5. What material predominates in DIN 127 washers and what hardness does it achieve?**  
The standard material is C67S spring steel hardened and tempered, with a surface hardness of 42 to 50 HRC; the A2 stainless steel versions have a hardness of 25 to 35 HRC / 255 to 335 HV.

**6. What is the service temperature limit of a carbon steel DIN 127 washer?**  
The carbon steel version retains its elastic properties up to 200 °C / 392 °F; above this temperature, temper loss and spring relaxation occur, while the A2 stainless steel variants can operate up to 300 °C / 572 °F.

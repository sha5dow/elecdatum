## Allowable Loads
The maximum service pressure in an NPT connection is not limited by the thread itself, but by the material and schedule of the pipe. As a reference, for Schedule 40 carbon steel pipe at room temperature, pressures on the order of 20.7 MPa (3,000 psi) up to 2″ diameters and 13.8 MPa (2,000 psi) for larger diameters can be handled, according to ASME B31.1. For different fluids and conditions, the corresponding reduction factors from the applicable piping standard must be applied. [VERIFY]

## Equivalences Between Standards (ANSI, ISO, DIN)
NPT threads are not interchangeable with other tapered threads, particularly BSPT (ISO 7-1, DIN 2999). Although both have a 1:16 taper, the flank angles and pitches differ, preventing any leak-tight mixed coupling.

| Characteristic | NPT (ANSI B1.20.1) | BSPT (ISO 7-1) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Taper | 1 in 16 (62.5 mm/m) | 1 in 16 (62.5 mm/m) |
| Flank angle | 60° | 55° |
| Crest and root | Flat | Rounded (sometimes) |
| Pitch (measured in) | Threads per inch (TPI) | Millimeters / threads per inch |
| Sealing | Requires sealant (tape or paste) | Requires sealant |
| Interchangeability with NPT | — | Not interchangeable |

*Example NPT series: Nominal size 1/2″ → 14 TPI, OD 0.840 in.*  
*Equivalent nominal BSPT 1/2″ → 14 TPI but OD 0.825 in and 55° profile, cannot be safely coupled.*  
*DIN 3852 designations (metric pipe threads) and the old DIN 2999 (BSPT) are also not compatible with NPT.*

## Design Characteristics
- **Taper**: The thread surface forms an angle of 1° 47′ 24″ (1.7899°) with the pipe axis, which is equivalent to a taper of 1 in 16 (3/4″ per foot or 62.5 mm/m).
- **Thread angle**: 60°, with flat truncated crests and roots.
- **Sealing**: Being tapered, the interference between crests and roots during tightening produces the mechanical seal. To ensure leak-tightness, a sealing compound (PTFE tape or paste) must be used.
- **Designation**: The external thread (male) is called MPT or NPT(M); the internal (female) FPT or NPT(F), although the ANSI standard only recognizes NPT. The usual nomenclature for a fitting is, for example, “1/2 – 14 NPT”.
- **NPTF**: Dryseal variant with controlled interference between crests and roots, defined in ANSI B1.20.3, which does not require sealant.

## Common Applications
- Water, compressed air, steam, and gas distribution systems in buildings and industry.
- Low and medium pressure hydraulic and pneumatic connections.
- Direct threading of steel, brass, PVC, and other materials in applications requiring occasional disassembly.
- Instrumentation and plumbing accessories (valves, filters, pressure gauges) under NPT standards.

## FAQ
**What is the taper of an NPT thread?**  
The taper is 1 in 16, which generates an angle of 1° 47′ 24″ (1.7899°) with respect to the pipe axis, equivalent to 62.5 mm per meter.

**How many threads per inch does a 1/2″ NPT thread have?**  
It has 14 threads per inch, corresponding to a pitch of 1.814 mm / 0.0714 in.

**What drill diameter is needed for tapping a 1/4″ NPT thread?**  
The recommended drill is 11.11 mm / 7/16 in for the corresponding tap.

**What is the nominal outer diameter of a 2″ NPT threaded pipe?**  
The nominal outer diameter is 60.33 mm / 2.375 in.

**What is the approximate thread length for a 3/4″ NPT thread?**  
Approximately 19.05 mm / 0.75 in, with a total of 10 threads cut.

**What tightening torque is recommended for a 1″ steel NPT fitting?**  
A typical assembly value is 81 N·m / 60 ft·lbf, always verifiable with the manufacturer's specifications.

To convert illuminance from lux (lx) to foot‑candle (fc, also abbreviated ft·cd), an exact conversion factor based on the relationship between the square meter and the square foot is used. One lux equals 0,09290304 foot‑candle, while one foot‑candle equals 10,7639104167 lux.

## Conversion Formula

The formula to obtain illuminance in foot‑candle from the value in lux is:

> **E<sub>fc</sub> = E<sub>lx</sub> × 0,09290304**

| Variable | Meaning | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E<sub>fc</sub> | Illuminance in foot‑candle | fc |
| E<sub>lx</sub> | Illuminance in lux | lx |
| 0,09290304 | Exact conversion factor (1/10,7639104167) | fc/lx |

It is also possible to express the conversion as division by the constant 10,7639104167:  
**E<sub>fc</sub> = E<sub>lx</sub> / 10,7639104167**

## Calculation Example

Calculate how many foot‑candle are equivalent to 500 lux.

1. Take the value in lux: 500 lx.
2. Apply the formula: E<sub>fc</sub> = 500 lx × 0,09290304 = 46,4515 fc.
3. Result: 500 lux correspond approximately to 46,45 foot‑candle.

For a quick estimate with ±10 % accuracy, it is enough to remember that 1 foot‑candle ≈ 10 lux.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### What is the difference between lux and foot‑candle?

The lux is the SI unit that measures illuminance, defined as one lumen per square meter. The foot‑candle is the US customary unit, equivalent to one lumen per square foot. The difference lies in the reference area: 1 m² vs. 1 ft².

### How much is 1 foot‑candle in lux?

1 foot‑candle is exactly equal to 10,7639104167 lux. In daily practice it is often rounded to 10,76 lux or even to 10,8 lux if high precision is not required.

### How many lux are 200 foot‑candle?

Applying the inverse of the formula: 200 fc × 10,7639104167 ≈ 2 152,8 lux. Therefore, 200 foot‑candle correspond to about 2 153 lux.

### What illuminance level is suitable for an office?

According to indoor lighting standards, an office with normal PC work and reading requires around 500 lux (approximately 46,5 fc). For detailed drafting tasks, between 1 000 and 2 000 lux (93–186 fc) are recommended.

### Can 10 lux = 1 foot‑candle be used as a rule of thumb?

Yes, for quick estimates with an error below 10 % it can be assumed that 10 lux ≈ 1 foot‑candle. This simplified equivalence should never be used in lighting design calculations that require precision.

### Why does the United States still use foot‑candle instead of lux?

The foot‑candle is part of the US customary system of units. Although lux is the internationally adopted unit, professionals in lighting, architecture, museum design, and horticulture in the United States continue to use the foot‑candle due to legacy standards and compatibility with local measuring equipment.

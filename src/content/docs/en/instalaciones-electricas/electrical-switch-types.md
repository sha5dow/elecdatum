Installation switches use more than 5 basic configurations, from single-pole to 4-way switching systems, all typically rated for 15 A and 20 A on 120 V circuits. These devices allow controlling electrical loads —mainly lighting— from one or several points, and are governed by standards such as the National Electrical Code (NEC) to ensure safe operation. The choice of the appropriate type depends on the number of control points, whether only on/off or dimming is required, and the environmental conditions where they will be installed. The incorporation of smart switches and electronic dimmers adds requirements for neutral and box fill, aspects that the NEC 2020 has reinforced.

## Switch Types
In residential and light commercial installations, 6 functional types of switches listed in the following table predominate. Each is described by its international nomenclature, number of poles and throws, and characteristic electrical parameters.

| Switch Type | Abbreviation (NEMA/IEC) | Poles / Throws | Typical Use | Rated Current | Rated Voltage | Recommended Copper Conductor Gauge | Rated Insulation Temperature |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Single-pole (SPST) | SPST (1P1T) | 1 pole, 1 throw | Control of one light from a single point | 15 A / 20 A | 120 V | 14 AWG (2.08 mm²) for 15 A; 12 AWG (3.31 mm²) for 20 A | 75 °C / 167 °F |
| Three-way switch (SPDT) | SPDT (1P2T) | 1 pole, 2 throws | Control from two points (e.g., hallways, stairs) | 15 A / 20 A | 120 V | 14 AWG (2.08 mm²) / 12 AWG (3.31 mm²) | 75 °C / 167 °F |
| Four-way switch | DPDT configured as intermediate | 2 poles, 2 throws | Control from three or more points, in combination with two three-way switches | 15 A / 20 A | 120 V | 14 AWG (2.08 mm²) / 12 AWG (3.31 mm²) | 75 °C / 167 °F |
| Double-pole (DPST) | DPST (2P1T) | 2 poles, 1 throw | Simultaneous disconnection of two phases (240 V loads, such as heaters) | 20 A / 30 A | 240 V | 12 AWG (3.31 mm²) / 10 AWG (5.26 mm²) | 75 °C / 167 °F |
| Dimmer | — | 1 pole, 1 throw or compatible with 3-way | Light intensity variation | 15 A / 20 A (or less depending on load type) | 120 V | 14 AWG (2.08 mm²) / 12 AWG (3.31 mm²) | 75 °C / 167 °F |
| Smart switch | — | Same as SPST or 3-way | Remote control, scheduling, home automation integration | 15 A normally | 120 V | 14 AWG (2.08 mm²) | 75 °C / 167 °F (per device) |

Most general-use switches are listed for tungsten, fluorescent or LED loads, and support at least 15 A. The operating temperature of the terminals is usually 75 °C / 167 °F, which conditions the conductor gauge.

## Operation and Wiring
The staircase switching system uses 2 three-way switches and 2 traveler conductors, allowing control from 2 different locations. In the standard configuration (traveler system), the live line (phase) is connected to the common terminal of one of the switches; the other two terminals of each switch are connected through the so-called travelers, and the common terminal of the second switch feeds the lamp. Operating either switch reverses the current path, turning the load on or off. The following table shows the four possible combinations, two with the lamp on and two off.

| Switch 1 Position | Switch 2 Position | Lamp State |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up | Up | Off |
| Up | Down | On |
| Down | Up | On |
| Down | Down | Off |

To control the same load from 3 or more points, four-way switches are added between the two three-way switches. Each four-way switch has two pairs of traveler terminals and is responsible for switching the straight or crossed line. In installations with control from three locations, two three-way switches and one four-way switch are used, and it can be expanded to 4 or more points by adding more four-way switches in between. There is no practical limit on quantity, although circuit length and voltage drop must be evaluated; in residential work, 3 intermediate switches (4 control points) are hardly ever exceeded.

## NEC Requirements for Switches
The NEC 2020 establishes a maximum operating height of 2.0 m / 6 ft 7 in, with no minimum height, and requires a neutral conductor in most lighting switch boxes. Below are the main points of Article 404 applicable to installation switches (non-industrial):

- **Actuation height:** the center of the handle in its highest position must not exceed 2.0 m / 6 ft 7 in above the floor or working platform [NEC 404.8(A)]. No minimum height is specified. A greater height is allowed if the switch is adjacent to the equipment it supplies and can be accessed by portable means.
- **Neutral in the box:** since the 2020 edition, all switches controlling line-to-neutral lighting loads must have a neutral conductor in the box, except when the installation meets one of the exceptions [404.2(C)]. For 3-way and 4-way switched circuits, it is sufficient for the neutral to be present in one of the boxes if the entire area of the space is visible from the switch positions.
- **Replacement of switches with electronics:** if a switch that requires a neutral connection (smart, timer, etc.) is installed in an existing installation without a neutral, it may be omitted provided it is not possible to add it without damaging finishes. In that case, the number of electronic devices without neutral may not exceed 5 per branch circuit nor 25 per feeder [404.22].
- **Box fill:** boxes containing switches may house splices that occupy at most 75% of the cross-sectional area at any point. If conductors pass through the box without splices, occupancy must not exceed 40% [404.3(B), referring to 312.8].
- **Grounding:** metallic yokes of switches and metallic faceplates must be connected to the circuit grounding conductor. In non-metallic boxes, the yoke is connected directly to the grounding conductor; in metallic boxes, fixing with metal screws ensures continuity [404.9(B)].
- **On/off indication:** general-use switches must clearly indicate the on and off position, usually by the toggle. Three-way and four-way switches are exempt, since the on position depends on the configuration of the other switch [404.7].
- **Installation in damp or wet locations:** any switch mounted on a surface or recessed in a damp or wet environment must be housed in a weatherproof enclosure or with appropriate protective cover [404.4]. It is not permitted to install switches inside bathtub or shower spaces, unless they are part of a listed assembly.

## Typical Applications
More than 90% of installation switches control lighting circuits in habitable rooms, stairs, and hallways. Three-way and four-way switches facilitate turning on and off from both ends of a long hallway or from the entrances of a large room, meeting the safety requirement that a control point is always within reach. Single-pole switches are used in individual rooms without the need for multiple points. Dimmers are installed in dining rooms, living rooms, and bedrooms to create ambiance, while smart switches add automation, voice control, and scheduling without additional rewiring when a neutral already exists in the box.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)
### How many 4-way switches can be installed between two 3-way switches?
There is no electrical limit; in residential practice, up to 3 crossover switches are used, allowing control of the same light from 4 or more points.

### What is the maximum height allowed for a wall switch according to NEC 2020?
The maximum height of the center of the handle in its highest position is 2.0 m / 6 ft 7 in above the floor, with no minimum height restriction [404.8(A)].

### What cable gauge is needed for a 20 A switch installed on a lighting circuit?
At a minimum, 12 AWG (3.31 mm²) copper conductor is required, capable of handling 20 A under a terminal temperature of 75 °C / 167 °F.

### How many neutral conductors must reach the boxes when using 3-way switches?
The NEC 2020 requires that at least one of the boxes of the switched circuit contain the neutral when the entire controlled area is visible from the switch positions [404.2(C) Ex.].

### What is the maximum fill percentage for a switch box with splices?
Splices must not occupy more than 75% of the wiring space in any cross-section, and conductors passing through without splices not more than 40% [312.8 / 404.3(B)].

### How many watts can a standard 15 A switch handle at 120 V?
For a purely resistive load, 1800 W (15 A × 120 V); for continuous lighting, it is recommended not to exceed 80% of its capacity, i.e., 1440 W.

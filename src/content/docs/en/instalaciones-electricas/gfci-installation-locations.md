The ground-fault circuit interrupter (GFCI) must be installed in all locations specified by the National Electrical Code (NEC) 2023 edition, particularly in sections 210.8(A) for dwellings and 210.8(B) for non-dwelling occupancies. GFCI protection is designed to disconnect the circuit when it detects a ground-fault current equal to or greater than 5 mA ± 1 mA, reducing the risk of electric shock to persons. The requirement covers 125 V to 250 V receptacles, single-phase circuits up to 50 A, and three-phase circuits up to 100 A, depending on the environment.

## Regulatory Requirements
GFCI protective devices must comply with Article 210.8 of the NEC 2023 and be installed in a readily accessible location. Protection may be provided by a combination type arc-fault circuit interrupter (AFCI) with integral GFCI or a GFCI receptacle. In the case of the receptacle, its load terminals can also protect downstream devices. The NEC establishes that the distance from a sink, bathtub, or shower stall is measured along the shortest path the equipment power cord would travel without passing through a floor, wall, ceiling, or fixed barrier. Starting with the 2023 edition, the exclusion for windows and doors was removed, meaning the measurement line may pass through them.

## Locations in One- and Multi-Family Dwellings
The following table summarizes areas within a dwelling unit where all receptacles must have GFCI protection, per NEC 2023 Section 210.8(A).

| Location | Applicable Distance |
|---|---|
| Bathrooms | Entire area |
| Garages and accessory buildings | Entire area |
| Outdoors | Entire area |
| Crawl spaces (at or below grade) | Entire area |
| Basements | Entire area |
| Kitchens | Entire area |
| Areas for food or beverage preparation or cooking | Entire area |
| Sinks | Receptacles within 1.83 m / 6 ft from the top inside edge of the bowl |
| Boat houses | Entire area |
| Bathtubs or showers (outside bathroom) | Receptacles within 1.83 m / 6 ft from the outside edge of the bathtub or shower |
| Laundry areas | Entire area |
| Indoor wet or damp locations | Entire area |

## Locations in Non-Dwelling Buildings
For occupancies other than dwellings, GFCI protection is required for 125 V to 250 V receptacles, single-phase circuits 50 A or less, and three-phase circuits 100 A or less, in the locations indicated below (NEC 210.8(B)).

| Location | Applicable Distance |
|---|---|
| Bathrooms | Entire area |
| Kitchens | Entire area |
| Areas for food or beverage preparation or cooking | Entire area |
| Buffet service areas (with permanent food or beverage provision) | Entire area |
| Rooftops | Entire area, with accessible access only from the rooftop itself |
| Outdoors | Entire area |
| Sinks | Receptacles within 1.83 m / 6 ft from the top inside edge; also applies to fixed or stationary equipment connected by cord and plug |
| Indoor wet or damp locations | Entire area |
| Locker rooms | Entire area |
| Garages, accessory buildings, and similar | Entire area |
| Crawl spaces (at or below grade) | Entire area |
| Unfinished areas of basements | Entire area |
| Aquatic tanks or fish tanks | Receptacles within 1.83 m / 6 ft from the top inside edge or the conductive support of the container |
| Laundry areas | Entire area |
| Bathtubs or showers (outside bathroom) | Receptacles within 1.83 m / 6 ft from the outside edge |

In addition, GFCI protection is required for 20 V lighting outlets in crawl spaces, and for various appliances listed in 210.8(D) even if hard-wired (e.g., vending machines, sump pumps, electric ranges).

## Exceptions to GFCI Protection
The NEC 2023 recognizes some limited exemptions from the general GFCI protection rule, including:

- A receptacle dedicated exclusively to fixed electric deicing equipment, provided it is not readily accessible and has ground-fault protection of equipment in accordance with 426.28 and 427.22.
- A receptacle that supplies only a permanently installed security system.
- A receptacle for a bathroom exhaust fan in a dwelling, unless the manufacturer's instructions indicate otherwise. If the fan is installed over the bathtub or shower footprint, GFCI is required per UL guide GPWX.

For rooftops in non-dwelling occupancies, GFCI receptacles need only be readily accessible from the rooftop itself.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)
### How far from a sink is GFCI protection required in a dwelling?
GFCI protection is required for all receptacles located at a distance equal to or less than 1.83 m / 6 ft from the top inside edge of the sink bowl, measured along the shortest path the power cord would travel.

### What is the maximum circuit current that requires GFCI in non-dwelling occupancies?
Single-phase circuits 50 A or less and three-phase circuits 100 A or less supplying 125 V to 250 V receptacles must have GFCI protection in the locations specified by the NEC.

### How far from a bathtub or shower is GFCI protection needed?
GFCI protection is required for any receptacle that is 1.83 m / 6 ft or less from the outside edge of the bathtub or shower, even if not installed in a bathroom.

### What distance from a receptacle to an aquarium requires GFCI?
The requirement applies to receptacles 1.83 m / 6 ft or less from the top inside edge or the conductive metal support of the tank, including fish tanks, bait tanks, or similar open aquatic containers.

### What leakage current trips a GFCI device?
The GFCI device interrupts the circuit when it detects a ground-fault current equal to or greater than 5 mA ± 1 mA, protecting persons against electric shock.

### In what type of crawl spaces is GFCI required for lighting?
GFCI protection is required for 20 V lighting outlets located in crawl spaces at or below grade, regardless of whether the space is used for storage or requires servicing.

Outdoor wiring comprises branch circuits and feeders installed outside buildings, with nominal voltages not exceeding 1000 V AC or 1500 V DC as defined by DIN VDE 0100 and NEC Article 225 standards. These circuits supply power to perimeter lighting, HVAC equipment, pumps, service poles, and detached structures such as garages or workshops, either through overhead, underground, or surface-mounted wiring directly exposed to weather.

## Regulatory framework

| Standard / Code | Scope | Reference document |
|---|---|---|
| NEC Article 225 | Requirements for outdoor branch circuits and feeders in the USA, including supports, clearances, overcurrent protection, and emergency disconnection | NFPA 70, 2023 Edition |
| DIN VDE 0100-738 | Installations in wet and damp areas and outdoor installations; specifies additional protections for environments with water presence | DIN VDE 0100 |
| NOM-001-SEDE | Electrical installations in Mexico; adopts equivalent guidelines to the NEC for outdoor wiring, raceways, and grounding | Official Gazette of the Federation |
| CEC Section 12-100 | Outdoor installations in Canada; distance, support, and raceway sealing requirements harmonized with the NEC | CSA C22.1 |

## Types of outdoor wiring systems

| System | Description | Typical application |
|---|---|---|
| Overhead wiring | Bare or insulated conductors supported by poles or brackets, with a clear span between structures | Power supply between separate buildings, public lighting |
| Underground wiring | Conductors buried directly or housed in PVC ducts, with required cover per zone | Power supply to gardens, parking lots, decorative posts |
| Surface wiring | Conductors in metallic or non-metallic raceway fixed to exterior walls, exposed to weather | Air conditioning units, pool pumps, perimeter lighting |
| Temporary wiring | Provisional installations with moisture-resistant flexible cables and mechanical protections | Construction sites, fairs, outdoor events |

## Components and materials

| Component | Material or type | Specification |
|---|---|---|
| Conductors | Copper or aluminum with moisture- and UV-resistant insulation | THWN-2, XHHW-2 or UF, with operating temperature of 90 °C / 194 °F |
| Raceways | Rigid PVC schedule 80, EMT, IMC, RMC or non-metallic LFNC | Impact resistance of 7 J / 5.2 ft·lb at -25 °C / -13 °F |
| Supports | Stainless steel 304 or hot-dip galvanized steel clamps | Spacing between supports per NEC 225, maximum 1.5 m / 5 ft for PVC |
| Junction boxes | Cast aluminum or polycarbonate watertight boxes, NEMA 3R or 4X | Ingress protection IP65 or higher, with hinged cover |
| Disconnects | Emergency switches, on-load contactors | Short-circuit current rating equal to or greater than available fault current, marked on red background with white letters at least 13 mm / 0.5 in |

## Installation requirements

Outdoor conductors shall be installed protected against physical damage and environmental conditions by metallic or non-metallic raceway approved for wet locations. Raceways on exterior surfaces must be secured with suitable supports at intervals not exceeding 1.5 m / 5 ft for PVC and 3 m / 10 ft for RMC. Cables exposed to sunlight shall be resistant to ultraviolet radiation or installed inside a conduit. Every point of entry of a raceway into a building shall be sealed with an approved compound to block moisture and pests, in accordance with NEC 225.27. Joints between conduit sections shall be made with watertight adapters and solvent cement compatible with the pipe material.

> **Voltage drop calculation**: **ΔV = (2 × L × I × R) / S**  
> where ΔV is the voltage drop in volts, L the circuit length in meters, I the load current in amperes, R the conductor resistance in ohms per meter, and S the temperature correction factor. The maximum recommended voltage drop is 3 % for feeder circuits and 5 % combined with branch circuits.

## Safety distances

| Installation condition | Minimum vertical distance | Minimum horizontal distance |
|---|---|---|
| Overhead conductors over sidewalks or pedestrian-traffic areas | 3.0 m / 10 ft | — |
| Overhead conductors over vehicle roads or parking lots | 5.5 m / 18 ft | — |
| Conductors over flat roofs with pedestrian access | 3.0 m / 10 ft | 1.2 m / 4 ft from roof edge |
| Conductors over sloped roofs with slope greater than 30° | 2.5 m / 8 ft | — |
| Separation from windows, doors, balconies, or emergency exits | — | 1.2 m / 4 ft (NEC 225.19) |

## Electrical protections

Every outdoor circuit must have overcurrent protection at its origin, sized at 125 % of the continuous load current per NEC 210.20. Ground-fault protection (GFCI) is required for outdoor receptacles with a trip threshold of 6 mA / 0.006 A, per NEC 210.8. Metallic raceways shall be connected to the grounding conductor using listed connectors. For one- and two-family dwellings, the 2023 NEC requires an emergency disconnect in an accessible, readily visible outdoor location (NEC 225.41), sized for the total service current and permanently marked with the legend "EMERGENCY DISCONNECT" on a red background with white text.

| Protection | Requirement | Applicable standard |
|---|---|---|
| Overcurrent | Circuit breaker or fuse at circuit origin, rating per conductor ampacity | NEC 240, DIN VDE 0100-430 |
| Ground fault | GFCI of 6 mA / 0.006 A at outdoor receptacles | NEC 210.8(A)(3) |
| Grounding | Connection of grounding conductor to main electrode with maximum resistance of 25 Ω | NEC 250, NOM-001 |
| Emergency disconnect | Visible, accessible, labeled disconnect device with available fault current interruption rating | NEC 225.41 |

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

1.  **What type of cable is required for an outdoor installation exposed to weather?** Cable with moisture- and sunlight-resistant insulation must be used, such as THWN-2 or XHHW-2; if directly buried, type UF cable with minimum conductor size of 2.05 mm / 12 AWG for 20 A circuits.
2.  **What is the minimum height for an overhead cable crossing a vehicle access?** The minimum height above the driving surface is 5.5 m / 18 ft, and 3.0 m / 10 ft over pedestrian sidewalks, per NEC 225.18.
3.  **Is an outdoor disconnect mandatory for dwellings?** Yes, since the 2020 edition of the NEC, an emergency outdoor disconnect is required for one- and two-family dwellings, located in an accessible and visible place from the dwelling unit.
4.  **Can NM-B cable be used outdoors?** No, NM-B cable is only approved for dry indoor locations. Outdoors, UF cable or XHHW-2 conductors inside a waterproof raceway approved for wet locations must be used.
5.  **How is an outdoor raceway entering a building sealed?** Neutral-cure silicone or polyurethane duct sealant is applied inside and outside the conduit end, forming a plug that blocks moisture, insects, and contaminants.
6.  **What is the minimum burial depth for an electrical cable in a residential garden?** For UF cable directly buried under non-vehicle areas, a minimum cover of 600 mm / 24 in is required; if housed in rigid PVC conduit, the cover can be reduced to 450 mm / 18 in per NEC Table 300.5.

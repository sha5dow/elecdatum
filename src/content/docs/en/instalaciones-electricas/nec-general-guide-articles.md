The National Electrical Code (NEC), or NFPA 70, is the most widely used US electrical safety standard, updated every 3 years and spanning over 1,000 pages. It establishes the rules for the safe installation of wiring and equipment in residential, commercial, and industrial occupancies, and is adopted by most states as the reference electrical code.

## Organization and structure

The NEC consists of an introduction, 9 chapters, informative annexes numbered A through J, and an index. The organization follows a logical progression:

- **Chapters 1 through 4** – General rules for all types of installations (definitions, basic requirements, circuits, overcurrent protection, grounding, wiring methods).
- **Chapters 5 through 7** – Special rules for occupancies, equipment, and particular conditions (hazardous locations, machinery, emergency systems).
- **Chapter 8** – Communication systems (telephone, radio, television, data networks).
- **Chapter 9** – Reference tables (conductor dimensions, resistances, raceway properties).

Each chapter is subdivided into articles, identified by three-digit numbers, which form the backbone of the code.

## Main NEC Articles

The following table lists the key articles that every professional should know, along with typical operating temperature values for insulation or equipment where applicable.

| Article | Title | Abbreviated description | Typical maximum operating temperature |
|---------|-------|-------------------------|---------------------------------------|
| 100 | Definitions | Fundamental terms used throughout the code. | – |
| 110 | Requirements for Electrical Installations | General conditions, nominal voltages, connections, working spaces. | 60 °C / 140 °F (terminals ≤100 A, unless otherwise indicated) |
| 200 | Use and Identification of Grounded Conductors | Standardized colors, neutral marking, minimum sizes. | – |
| 210 | Branch Circuits | Classification, number of outlets, GFCI/AFCI protection, permissible loads. | – |
| 250 | Grounding and Bonding | Grounding electrodes, grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, maximum resistance. | – |
| 300 | Wiring Methods | Installation of conductors, raceways, cable trays, support distances, bends. | – |
| 310 | Conductors for General Installations | Insulation types (TW, THW, THHN…), ampacity tables, correction factors. | 60 °C / 140 °F, 75 °C / 167 °F, 90 °C / 194 °F (depending on insulation type) |
| 400 | Flexible Cables and Cords | Permitted uses, ampacity, gauges, designations (SJ, SO, SJO…). | 60 °C / 140 °F (typical for flexible cords) |
| 430 | Motors, Motor Circuits, and Controllers | Overcurrent protection, conductor sizing, and disconnecting means. | – |
| 500 | Hazardous (Classified) Locations | Class I, II, III; Division 1 and 2; gas groups; surface temperature limits. | T4 limit: 135 °C / 275 °F (for atmospheres with Group C and D gases) |

The indicated temperatures are the standardized maximum values for the insulation or equipment surface under continuous operating conditions, according to the ampacity tables and requirements of each article.

## Adoption of the NEC by State

The NEC is not a federal law; each state or local jurisdiction decides which edition to adopt and when it becomes effective. The current landscape shows widespread but staggered adoption:

- More than 30 states have adopted the 2020 or 2023 edition, with effective dates ranging from 2022 to 2026.
- Some states, such as California and Texas, already enforce the 2023 edition; others like Florida and New York are in the process of transitioning.
- A few (Indiana, Tennessee, Wisconsin) still maintain earlier editions (2008 or 2017) with their own amendments.
- In states without state adoption (Arizona, Illinois, Kansas, Missouri), each municipality chooses its own regulations.

The authority having jurisdiction (AHJ) is responsible for inspecting compliance with the locally adopted code.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### How often is a new edition of the NEC published?
The NFPA publishes a new edition of the NEC every 3 years; the most recent version is the 2023 edition and totals over 1,000 pages of requirements.

### How many chapters make up the NEC?
The code is structured into 9 chapters, preceded by an introduction and followed by 10 informative annexes (A–J) and an alphabetical index.

### Which article defines the current-carrying capacity of conductors?
Article 310 establishes the ampacity tables, with maximum insulation temperatures of 60 °C / 140 °F, 75 °C / 167 °F, and 90 °C / 194 °F, depending on the conductor type.

### How many states have adopted the 2023 edition of the NEC as of 2025?
More than 20 states have already enacted the 2023 edition, with effective dates covering the period 2023‑2025, according to Mike Holt's tracking records.

### Which article is dedicated to overcurrent protection?
Article 240 exclusively covers overcurrent protection, encompassing fuses and circuit breakers with rated capacities from 15 A to 6,000 A.

### What surface temperature limit does the NEC impose for Class I explosive atmospheres?
Article 500 defines temperature classes; for example, T4 class limits the maximum surface temperature to 135 °C / 275 °F, suitable for most gases in Groups C and D.

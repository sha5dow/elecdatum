A meter base is the standardized support where the electric energy meter is housed and connected, providing the mechanical and electrical interface between the utility service drop and the user's installation. Its design must comply with requirements for mechanical strength, weather protection, accessibility for reading and maintenance, and safe work spaces in accordance with current electrical codes.

## Work Spaces
The depth of the work space in front of a meter base is measured from the front face of the enclosure and depends on the nominal voltage to ground and the installation conditions. When the base requires examination, adjustment, or maintenance while energized, the indicated minimum distances must be respected.

| Nominal Voltage to Ground | Condition 1 (exposed live parts on one side, no grounded parts on the other) | Condition 2 (exposed live parts on one side, grounded parts on the other) | Condition 3 (exposed live parts on both sides) |
|---------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| 0 – 150 V                 | 0.91 m / 3 ft                                                               | 0.91 m / 3 ft                                                            | 0.91 m / 3 ft                                 |
| 151 – 600 V               | 0.91 m / 3 ft                                                               | 1.07 m / 3.5 ft                                                          | 1.22 m / 4 ft                                 |

The width of the work space must be at least 762 mm (30 inches) or the total width of the equipment, whichever is greater. The height of the work space must extend from the floor or platform to 1.98 m (6.5 ft) or the height of the equipment, whichever is larger, and no object may be stored in that entire volume. When the equipment is installed in a corridor and exposed live parts during inspection or service, the work space must be protected from the traffic of unauthorized persons.

## Mounting Height
The installation height of the meter base is generally defined by taking the center of the device as a reference. It is recommended to place it between 1.5 m and 1.8 m (5 ft to 6 ft) above the finished floor level to facilitate visual reading and safe handling. When the base is located outdoors, it must be ensured that the lower edge of the base is at least 0.3 m / 12 in above the expected snow line or typical flood level, according to local conditions [VERIFY].

## Location Requirements
The base must be placed on a wall or fixed structure, accessible from the outside or from a common electrical service area. In single-family homes it is usually installed on the front or side facade, ensuring a direct route to the service pole. The base may not be mounted on combustible surfaces without adequate protection, and a minimum horizontal distance of 1.0 m (3.3 ft) from windows, doors, gas meters, or ignition sources must be maintained.

## Materials and Construction
Meter bases are typically made of painted galvanized steel, cast aluminum, or UV-resistant polycarbonate. They must meet the minimum IP protection rating required for the location (generally IP44 for outdoor under cover or IP54 for direct exposure to weather). Brackets and fastening hardware shall be stainless steel or galvanized to prevent galvanic corrosion, and the base must include provision for sealing by the electric utility company.

## Connections and Grounding
The base incorporates input (line) and output (load) terminals sized according to the service capacity, commonly 100 A, 200 A, or 400 A. All terminals must accept copper or aluminum conductors of the corresponding gauge, and a specific terminal is required for the connection of the installation's grounding electrode conductor. Connections are tightened to the torque specified by the manufacturer, and the base is firmly connected to the building grounding system by a continuous copper conductor, bare or insulated with green/yellow identification.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)
### What is the minimum required front clear distance for a 240 V meter base in a residential installation?
The minimum front clear distance required according to electrical installation standards is 0.91 m (3 feet) from the front face of the enclosure to any obstacle, applicable for nominal voltages to ground up to 150 V in condition 1 or 2.

### What is the minimum width required for the work space in front of a meter base?
The minimum width of the work space is 762 mm (30 inches) measured horizontally, which may be centered relative to the equipment or aligned to one of its sides, provided it is not less than the width of the cabinet itself.

### At what maximum height can a combined load center with meter base be installed?
The maximum height of the meter center for direct reading without aids is 1.8 m (6 feet) above the floor, although standards allow up to 2.0 m (6.5 ft) if the equipment is accessible by a portable platform.

### How much vertical clear space must be above a meter base?
A continuous vertical clear space of at least 1.98 m (6.5 feet) from the floor to the height defined for the work space is required, including the area above the meter, unless the equipment exceeds that elevation, in which case the equipment height defines the limit.

### What distance must a meter base maintain from a gas pipe?
The minimum horizontal distance between an electric meter base and a gas meter or fuel gas pipe is 1.0 m (3.3 feet) to reduce ignition risks and allow safe operations on both installations.

### How much may a structural support or other equipment protrude in front of the meter base?
Adjacent supports, trays, conduits, or other equipment shall not protrude more than 150 mm (6 inches) from the front plane of the meter base, so as not to invade the work space or obstruct the opening of doors to 90 degrees.

## Overview
MIG/MAG (GMAW) welding wire is a continuous consumable electrode supplied in coils and spools, designed to be automatically fed through the torch. Its diameter is standardized according to specifications such as AWS A5.18, A5.9, and A5.10, ranging from 0.6 mm / 0.023 in to 2.4 mm / 0.094 in in the heaviest applications. Wire selection depends on the base metal to be welded, welding position, transfer mode, and shielding gas used. The specifications cover carbon steels, stainless steels, aluminum, and other alloys, ensuring defined mechanical properties and metallurgical compatibility.

## Applications
MIG welding with solid wire is used in the following industries and components:
- **Automotive and bodywork**: joining carbon steel and aluminum sheets in frames, panels, and exhausts.
- **Metal structure fabrication**: beams, columns, and supports with structural steels.
- **Food and chemical processing equipment**: stainless steel 304L/316L pipes and tanks, where corrosion resistance is required.
- **Shipbuilding and marine industry**: repair of hulls and aluminum components exposed to saline environments.
- **Process piping and pressure vessels**: root and fill welds with high toughness in carbon steels.
- **General maintenance**: quick repairs on thin and medium thicknesses using short circuit transfer.

**Technical notes:**
- Short circuit transfer is used on thin thicknesses (≤4 mm / 0.16 in) and out-of-position welding.
- Pulsed spray transfer minimizes spatter and allows welding aluminum and stainless steel in all positions.
- For aluminum wires, it is essential to use push-pull torches or spool guns to avoid feeding problems.
- Diameter tolerances for GMAW wires are typically ±0.01 mm / ±0.0004 in per AWS A5.18/A5.9/A5.10.

## Advantages and Limitations
- **Advantages**
  - Wide range of alloys available for carbon steels, stainless steels, aluminum, nickel, and copper.
  - High productivity due to continuous wire feeding, ideal for robotic automation.
  - Spray transfer mode provides high penetration and smooth beads without spatter.
  - Copper-coated wires (e.g., ER70S-6) improve electrical conductivity and oxidation resistance during storage.
  - Low carbon (L) versions and stabilizers (Si, Mn) minimize porosity and increase deposit toughness.
- **Limitations**
  - Susceptibility to drafts outdoors, which can disperse the shielding gas and cause defects.
  - Aluminum wires require special push-pull feeding equipment to avoid tangling and deformation.
  - Stainless steel wires require very strict heat input control to avoid distortion and loss of corrosion-resistant properties.
  - Solid wire cannot be used without external gas, unlike self-shielded flux-cored wires.

## Selection Guide
| Base metal | Recommended MIG wire | Remarks |
|------------|----------------------|---------|
| Carbon steel (SAE 1010 – 1045) | ER70S-6 | Versatile for boiler making and automotive; supports short circuit and spray transfer. |
| High-strength low-alloy steel (HSLA) | ER80S-D2, ER100S-1 | Requires preheating (150–200 °C / 302–392 °F) and hydrogen control. |
| Stainless steel 304 / 304L | ER308L | Excellent corrosion resistance; use low CO₂ gas (<5%) to maintain low carbon. |
| Stainless steel 316 / 316L | ER316L | Contains molybdenum (2–3%) for chloride resistance; typical in chemical and marine industry. |
| Aluminum 3003, 6061, 6063 series | ER4043 | Reduces hot cracking tendency; do not use on alloys requiring decorative anodizing. |
| Aluminum 5052, 5083 series | ER5356 | Higher tensile strength (≥240 MPa / 35 ksi) and excellent low-temperature toughness. |
| Deoxidized copper | ERCu | Requires intense preheating (400–600 °C / 752–1112 °F); 100% Ar gas. |
| Dissimilar carbon steel – stainless steel joint | ER309L | Provides higher chromium and nickel content to avoid brittle martensite in the dilution zone. |

For thin thicknesses (≤ 3 mm / 0.12 in) in any material, wire of 0.8–0.9 mm / 0.030–0.035 in is used with short circuit transfer. For medium and thick thicknesses, diameters of 1.2 mm / 0.045 in or larger and spray transfer are chosen.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)
**1. What is the minimum tensile strength of ER70S-6 wire?**  
The minimum tensile strength specified by AWS A5.18 is 480 MPa / 70 ksi, although typical values range between 500 and 560 MPa / 72 and 81 ksi when using an argon/CO₂ gas mixture.

**2. What wire diameters are most common for welding aluminum with ER4043?**  
The most commonly used diameters are 1.2 mm / 0.047 in and 1.6 mm / 0.063 in; for thicknesses above 6 mm / 0.24 in, 2.4 mm / 0.094 in is preferred, always using special feeding equipment.

**3. What preheat temperature should be applied to a low-alloy steel before using ER100S-1?**  
Preheating between 150 °C / 302 °F and 200 °C / 392 °F is recommended, depending on thickness and carbon equivalent, to avoid hydrogen cracking.

**4. What arc length is maintained when welding stainless steel with short circuit transfer and 1.0 mm wire?**  
The ideal arc length is approximately 3 to 6 mm / 0.12 to 0.24 in, equivalent to a voltage of 17–20 V, which produces stable transfer and low spatter.

**5. What minimum elongation is required for the weld deposit of an ER308L wire?**  
AWS A5.9 requires a minimum elongation of 35%, ensuring adequate ductility for subsequent forming and cold service.

**6. What shielding gas flow rate is normally set when welding aluminum with 1.6 mm wire?**  
A flow rate of 15 to 20 L/min / 32 to 42 ft³/h of pure argon is established, sufficient to maintain gas coverage without generating turbulence that could introduce air.

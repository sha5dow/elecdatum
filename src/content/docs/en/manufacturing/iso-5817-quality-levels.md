## General description
The international standard ISO 5817 establishes three quality levels —B, C and D— for imperfections in fusion-welded joints of metallic materials, excluding energy beam welding. Level B represents the highest requirement and is reserved for components subjected to critical loads or fatigue; level C is the intermediate standard for general use in metal structures; level D corresponds to basic requirements where only static strength is required. Evaluation is based on visual inspection and non-destructive testing, establishing quantitative limits for each type of imperfection as a function of weld thickness. The standard covers welds with typical thicknesses between 0,5 mm / 0.02 in and 63 mm / 2.48 in, and is applicable to most arc and autogenous welding processes.

## Operating parameters
The quality level rating depends on the magnitude of detected imperfections against the tabulated limits for the sheet or tube thickness. The main parameters are the type of imperfection, its actual dimension, and the frequency of occurrence along 100 mm / 3.94 in of weld. Acceptance values are expressed in millimeters or as a percentage of the nominal joint thickness. The following table lists the maximum limits for some frequent imperfections in butt joints of 10 mm / 0.39 in thickness.

| Imperfection | Level B | Level C | Level D |
|--------------|---------|---------|---------|
| Continuous undercut (depth) | Not permitted | 0,5 mm / 0.02 in | 1,0 mm / 0.04 in |
| Lack of penetration (height) | Not permitted | 0,3 mm / 0.012 in | 0,6 mm / 0.024 in |
| Isolated porosity (diameter) | 0,2 mm / 0.008 in | 0,4 mm / 0.016 in | 0,6 mm / 0.024 in |
| Spatter (number per 100 mm / 3.94 in) | 1 | 3 | 5 |
| Excess reinforcement (height) | 1,0 mm / 0.04 in | 1,5 mm / 0.06 in | 2,5 mm / 0.1 in |
| Angular distortion (°) | 0,5 | 1,0 | 2,0 |

The inspection frequency is defined in the inspection plan: for level B, NDT is usually applied to 100% of the welded length, while for level D, sampling every 500 mm / 19.69 in or less than 5% of the joints may suffice.

## Typical tolerances
Allowable tolerances for weld dimensions and geometric defects are directly linked to the quality level. At level B, almost all surface irregularities are eliminated; at level C, small discontinuities without acute stress concentration are permitted; level D accepts visible imperfections provided they do not compromise static strength. The most commonly used reference values for structural steel welds of 15 mm / 0.59 in are:

| Geometric parameter | Level B | Level C | Level D |
|-----------------------|---------|---------|---------|
| Edge misalignment | 0,3 mm / 0.012 in | 0,6 mm / 0.024 in | 1,0 mm / 0.04 in |
| Maximum weld reinforcement | 1,0 mm / 0.04 in | 1,5 mm / 0.06 in | 2,5 mm / 0.1 in |
| Undercut (mordedura) max. depth | 0,2 mm / 0.008 in | 0,5 mm / 0.02 in | 1,0 mm / 0.04 in |
| Maximum angular deviation | 0,5° | 0,75° | 1,5° |
| Maximum isolated pore diameter | 0,3 mm / 0.012 in | 0,5 mm / 0.02 in | 0,8 mm / 0.031 in |
| Maximum lack of fusion length | 0 mm / 0 in | 0,3 mm / 0.012 in | 0,6 mm / 0.024 in |

In austenitic stainless steels type 304 or 316, the limits for levels C and D may be slightly adjusted to avoid areas with excessive heat that deteriorate intergranular corrosion resistance, especially in thicknesses above 6 mm / 0.24 in.

## Applicable materials
ISO 5817 is applicable to all metallic materials used in fusion-welded joints, provided the welding process is within the scope of the standard. The most common material groups are carbon steels, low-alloy steels, stainless steels (austenitic, ferritic, and duplex), and aluminum alloys. The intrinsic weldability of each material influences the ease of achieving levels B or C, as shown in the qualitative evaluation (scale 1 = not recommended, 10 = excellent) for different processes:

| Material | Arc welding (SMAW/GMAW) | Resistance welding | Brazing |
|----------|-------------------------------|---------------------------|-------------------|
| Carbon steel (low alloy) | 10 | 10 | 10 |
| Stainless steel 304/316 | 10 | 10 | 10 |
| Aluminum series 5000/6000 | 7 | 7 | 7 |
| Titanium | 7 | 3 | 3 |
| Gray cast iron | 7 | 1 | 3 |

Austenitic stainless steels specified in the AISI classification, such as 304 (UNS S30400) and 316 (UNS S31600), exhibit excellent weldability and are routinely welded at level C without special precautions. However, to achieve level B in these materials, rigorous control of heat input (typically between 0,5 kJ/mm and 2,5 kJ/mm / 12.7 kJ/in to 63.5 kJ/in) and adequate protection against hydrogen contamination are required.

## Advantages and limitations
The main advantage of ISO 5817 classification is that it provides a common language among designers, manufacturers, and inspectors, reducing rejections due to discrepancies in the interpretation of imperfections by 30%. Additionally, it allows selecting the optimal quality level based on the criticality of the component, avoiding overspecifications that increase manufacturing costs. As a limitation, the standard does not cover high-energy beam welding processes (laser, electron beam), which require ISO 13919, and its application requires specific training of the inspector to properly evaluate certain imperfections such as elongated slag inclusions. It also does not define acceptance criteria for welds of dissimilar materials, which must be agreed between client and supplier. In structures exposed to fatigue or cryogenics, level B does not always guarantee the desired service life and must be complemented with fracture mechanics analysis.

## Selection guide
The choice of quality level is based on the service requirement, required safety, and inspection cost. The following table summarizes the typical assignment for different applications.

| Application | Recommended level | Relative inspection cost | Remarks |
|------------|-------------------|------------------------------|---------------|
| Pressure vessels (PED) | B | High (x3.5) | Requires additional NDT |
| Building structures (EN 1090) | C | Medium (x1.8) | For execution class EXC2 |
| Cladding and metal joinery | D | Basic (x1.0) | Visual inspection only |
| Process piping (non-hazardous fluids) | C / D | Medium-low | D for diameters ≤ 50 mm / 1.97 in |
| Aerospace components | B+ additional requirements | Very high | Supplemented with ISO 10042 for aluminum |
| Food processing equipment (stainless steel) | C | Medium | Additional hygienic requirement |

In the food industry, level C is preferred combined with a polished surface finish (Ra ≤ 0,8 µm / 31.5 µin) to prevent bacteria accumulation in pores or spatter. ISO 5817 does not directly address surface roughness, so additional requirements must be specified.

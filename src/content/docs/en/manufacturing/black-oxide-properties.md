## General properties

Black oxide is a chemical conversion coating that transforms the metal surface into magnetite (Fe₃O₄), generating a layer of typical thickness 1 µm / 0.00004 in. This process is applied at temperatures between 20 °C and 141 °C / 68 °F and 286 °F depending on the variant used, and does not produce significant dimensional buildup on treated parts.

The formed magnetite layer is mechanically more stable and provides better corrosion protection than red oxide (rust, Fe₂O₃). However, freshly formed black oxide is porous and therefore requires a subsequent sealing with oil, wax, or lacquer to achieve its maximum corrosion resistance.

| Property | Typical value | Observations |
|---|---|---|
| Layer thickness | 1 µm / 0.00004 in | Minimal dimensional buildup |
| Chemical composition | Magnetite (Fe₃O₄) | Hot and medium temperature processes |
| Composition (cold) | Copper selenide (Cu₂Se) | Not a conversion coating |
| Hot process temperature | 141 °C / 286 °F | Sodium hydroxide bath with nitrates/nitrites |
| Medium temperature | 90–120 °C / 194–248 °F | Below boiling point |
| Cold temperature | 20–30 °C / 68–86 °F | Room temperature process |
| Corrosion resistance | Light to moderate | Requires oil or wax impregnation |
| Surface finish | Black matte to semi-gloss | Minimizes light reflection |
| Surface hardness | Does not increase significantly | Conversion coating, not deposition |
| Adhesion | Excellent | Forms from the base metal surface |

## Parameters by material

Process parameters vary according to base material and type of black oxide applied. Specification MIL-DTL-13924 covers four process classes for different substrates, while AMS 2485 and ASTM D769 provide alternative standards for aerospace and industrial applications.

| Base material | Recommended process | Chemical bath temperature | Immersion time (min) | Main chemical agents | Required post-treatment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Carbon steel | Hot | 140–141 °C / 284–286 °F | 5–30 | NaOH + NaNO₃ + NaNO₂ | Oil or wax |
| Alloy steel | Hot | 140–141 °C / 284–286 °F | 10–45 | NaOH + NaNO₃ + NaNO₂ | Oil or wax |
| Stainless steel | Hot | 140–141 °C / 284–286 °F | 10–60 | NaOH + NaNO₃ + NaNO₂ (special formulation) | Oil or wax |
| Carbon steel | Medium temperature | 90–120 °C / 194–248 °F | 5–30 | Alkaline salts | Oil or wax |
| Carbon steel | Cold | 20–30 °C / 68–86 °F | 3–10 | Selenium-copper compounds | Oil, wax, or lacquer |
| Copper and alloys | Hot | 140–141 °C / 284–286 °F | 5–20 | Specific formulation for copper | Oil or wax |
| Zinc and alloys | Hot | 140–141 °C / 284–286 °F | 3–15 | Specific formulation for zinc | Oil or wax |
| Powder metallurgy metals | Hot or medium | 90–141 °C / 194–286 °F | 10–40 | According to base material | Oil impregnation |

### Surface preparation prior

Surface preparation is critical for finish uniformity. Parts must be cleaned in an alkaline detergent bath to remove oils, greases, and contaminants, followed by water rinsing. Any rust or mill scale residues must be removed by acid pickling or blasting before treatment.

### Chemical bath control

In the hot process, the sodium hydroxide (NaOH) bath with nitrates and nitrites operates at 141 °C / 286 °F. Water must be added periodically to replenish evaporation losses, with strict controls to prevent steam explosions. NaOH concentration is typically maintained in the range of 40–50% by weight.

## Advantages

- High-volume production, ideal for small parts such as screws, bolts, and stamped components
- No significant dimensional impact: layer thickness of 1 µm / 0.00004 in does not affect tight tolerances
- Lower cost compared to equivalent protection systems such as paint or electroplating
- Does not cause hydrogen embrittlement, unlike electrodeposition processes
- Maintains the mechanical properties of the base material without altering its metallurgical structure
- Excellent adhesion because it forms by chemical conversion of the surface, not by deposition
- Uniform black decorative finish that reduces glare and eye fatigue
- Compatible with carbon steels, alloy steels, stainless steel, copper, zinc, and powder metallurgy metals
- Process applicable in facilities with conventional immersion tank equipment
- Post-treatment with oil or wax allows adjusting the final level of anticorrosive protection

## Limitations

- Limited corrosion resistance without subsequent sealing with oil, wax, or lacquer
- Hot process operates at 140–141 °C / 284–286 °F, requiring rigorous safety controls due to steam explosion risk
- Cold variant produces a copper selenide coating that tends to flake off easily and offers lower abrasion resistance
- Hot process generates caustic vapors that require adequate extraction and ventilation systems
- Not suitable for high corrosion resistance applications without complementary coatings
- Color limitations: only produces black finish; not available in other colors
- Finish quality critically depends on surface preparation and bath conditions
- Effluent treatment is necessary due to the toxicity of the nitrate, nitrite, and selenium compounds used
- Parts with complex geometries or deep cavities may exhibit coating irregularities

## FAQ

### What is the thickness of the black oxide layer?

The black oxide layer formed by chemical conversion has a typical thickness of 1 µm / 0.00004 in, which represents minimal dimensional buildup that does not affect tight tolerances on machined parts.

### At what temperature is black oxide applied?

The hot black oxide process is applied at 141 °C / 286 °F, while the medium temperature variant operates between 90 and 120 °C / 194 and 248 °F and the cold process is performed at room temperature between 20 and 30 °C / 68 and 86 °F.

### How long does the anticorrosive protection of black oxide last?

Without subsequent sealing, black oxide provides corrosion resistance of only 24 to 48 hours in indoor environment; with oil or wax impregnation, protection extends to hundreds of hours in salt spray testing per ASTM B117 specification.

### What metals can be treated with black oxide?

Black oxide is applicable to more than 5 families of materials: carbon steels, alloy steels, stainless steel, copper and its alloys, zinc, and powder metallurgy metals; it is not suitable for aluminum or titanium.

### What military standards cover black oxide?

Specification MIL-DTL-13924 is the oldest and most widely used military standard, covering 4 process classes for different substrates; AMS 2485 and ASTM D769 are complementary specifications for aerospace and industrial applications.

### What is the difference between hot and cold black oxide?

The hot process converts the surface into magnetite (Fe₃O₄) at 141 °C / 286 °F and offers higher abrasion resistance, while the cold process deposits copper selenide (Cu₂Se) at 20–30 °C / 68–86 °F and tends to flake off easily, requiring mandatory sealing with oil, wax, or lacquer.

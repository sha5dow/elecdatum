## Chemical composition
PVC contains 57% by mass of chlorine in its polymer structure. It is an amorphous thermoplastic derived from the polymerization of vinyl chloride monomer (CH₂=CHCl), yielding the repeat unit –(CH₂–CHCl)ₙ–. Its IUPAC name is poly(1‑chloroethylene) and the CAS number is 9002‑86‑2.

| Component / Parameter        | Value            |
|------------------------------|------------------|
| Chemical formula             | (C₂H₃Cl)ₙ       |
| Chlorine content (approx.)   | 57 % by mass     |
| CAS number                   | 9002‑86‑2        |
| Appearance (pure powder)     | White, brittle solid |
| Odor                         | Odorless          |

## Mechanical properties
The tensile strength of rigid unplasticized PVC reaches 51 MPa. Mechanical properties vary significantly with formulation; typical values for the rigid grade (UPVC) are presented below based on the AZoM source, complemented with impact data from the literature. Plasticized PVC shows much lower strengths and moduli, while elongation can exceed 300 %.

| Property                                   | Value (SI)       | Value (Imperial)       |
|---------------------------------------------|------------------|------------------------|
| Tensile strength                            | 51 MPa           | 7,400 psi              |
| Flexural modulus                            | 3.0 GPa          | 435 ksi                |
| Elongation at break                         | 60 %             | 60 %                   |
| Yield strain                                | 3.5 %            | 3.5 %                  |
| Izod impact strength (notched)              | 2 – 5 kJ/m²      | 0.95 – 2.38 ft·lb/in   |
| Surface hardness (Rockwell R)               | RR 110           | –                      |

*Note: The Izod impact strength comes from standardized sources; the value of 0.08 kJ/m reported by AZoM is not representative of the typical material behavior.*

## Physical properties
The density of rigid PVC is 1.4 g/cm³ (87.4 lb/ft³). It is a material that absorbs very little water and exhibits good dimensional stability, although its coefficient of thermal expansion is moderately high for a plastic.

| Property                                   | Value (SI)             | Value (Imperial)       |
|---------------------------------------------|------------------------|------------------------|
| Density                                     | 1.4 g/cm³              | 87.4 lb/ft³            |
| Water absorption (24 h)                     | 0.1 %                  | 0.1 %                  |
| Linear thermal expansion coefficient        | 6 × 10⁻⁵ / °C          | 3.3 × 10⁻⁵ / °F        |
| Oxygen index                                | 45 %                   | 45 %                   |
| UL 94 flammability rating                   | V‑0                    | V‑0                    |
| Solubility (approx.)                        | Insoluble in water, ethanol; slightly soluble in THF, DMF, ketones |

## Thermal properties
The maximum continuous service temperature of rigid PVC is 50 °C (120 °F). Above this value the material loses mechanical properties appreciably; for pipe applications a service temperature up to 60 °C is permitted with a significant reduction in working pressure.

| Property                                   | Value (SI)             | Value (Imperial)         |
|---------------------------------------------|------------------------|--------------------------|
| Maximum continuous service temperature      | 50 °C                  | 120 °F                   |
| Maximum temperature in pipes (PVC-U)        | 60 °C *                | 140 °F *                 |
| Glass transition temperature (Tg)           | 82 °C                  | 180 °F                   |
| Melting range for processing                | 150 – 220 °C           | 300 – 430 °F             |
| HDT at 0.45 MPa                             | 70 °C                  | 158 °F                   |
| HDT at 1.80 MPa                             | 67 °C                  | 153 °F                   |
| Specific heat                               | 0.9 kJ/(kg·K)          | 0.21 Btu/(lb·°F)         |
| Effective heat of combustion                | 17.95 MJ/kg            | 7,720 Btu/lb             |

*With a pressure reduction factor of 0.22 at 60 °C per Engineering Toolbox.*

## Electrical properties
The volume resistivity of rigid PVC reaches 10¹⁴ Ω·cm, making it an excellent insulator. Its dielectric strength is high and dielectric losses remain low at mains frequency.

| Property                                   | Value (SI)             | Value (Imperial)       |
|---------------------------------------------|------------------------|------------------------|
| Volume resistivity (log Ω·cm)               | 14                     | –                      |
| Dielectric strength                         | 40 MV/m *              | 1,000 V/mil *          |
| Dissipation factor (1 kHz)                  | 0.025                  | 0.025                  |
| Dielectric constant (1 kHz)                 | 3.1                    | 3.1                    |

*According to Wikipedia (40 MV/m); AZoM reports 14 MV/m for the grade tested; the typical value of the material is in the high range.*

## Chemical resistance
PVC shows good general resistance to acids, alkalis and salt solutions. However, it is attacked by organic solvents, especially chlorinated hydrocarbons, ketones and aromatic derivatives, which can cause stress cracking.

| Agent / Medium                           | Behavior                          |
|------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Dilute acids and bases                   | Excellent resistance              |
| Salt solutions                           | Excellent resistance              |
| Chlorinated solvents and ketones         | Severe attack, risk of stress cracking |
| Alcohols                                 | Good resistance (ethanol, etc.)   |
| Water (up to 50 °C)                      | Very low absorption, good behavior|
| UV radiation                             | Good intrinsic resistance         |

## Processing
The molding temperature is usually between 150 and 220 °C. Because PVC without additives decomposes before melting, the use of thermal stabilizers is essential, and for many applications impact modifiers or plasticizers are used to facilitate flow.

| Processing parameter (injection)       | Value (SI)         | Value (Imperial)     |
|----------------------------------------|--------------------|----------------------|
| Recommended melt temperature           | 150 – 220 °C       | 300 – 430 °F         |
| Mold temperature                       | 20 – 40 °C         | 68 – 104 °F          |
| Mold shrinkage                         | 0.4 %              | 0.004 in/in          |
| Pre-drying (hours @ temperature)       | 2 h @ 75 °C        | 2 h @ 167 °F         |

## Applications
With an approximate global production of 40 million tons per year, PVC is primarily used in construction. In its rigid form it is used in pipes, window profiles and cladding; plasticized, it is used in cable insulation, flooring, signage and inflatable products.

- **Construction**: Pressure water pipes and sanitary pipes, gutters, doors and windows, cladding.
- **Packaging**: Rigid bottles for non-food products, films.
- **Electrical and electronics**: Insulation of electrical conductors (plasticized formulation).
- **Consumer and other**: Vinyl records, credit cards, flooring, synthetic leather.

## Advantages and disadvantages
PVC is one of the most economical and versatile thermoplastics; its main strength lies in the cost-strength ratio and inherent flame retardancy. Restrictions come from processing and the low service temperature compared to engineering plastics.

**Advantages**  
- Low raw material cost.  
- High stiffness and mechanical strength.  
- Self-extinguishing (oxygen index 45%).  
- Good weather resistance (UV) and vapor barrier.  
- Excellent resistance to acids and alkalis.

**Disadvantages**  
- Difficult processing without stabilizers; it degrades before melting.  
- Low resistance to stress cracking in the presence of solvents.  
- Brittleness below 5 °C without impact modification.  
- Continuous service temperature limited to 50 °C (120 °F).  
- Emission of HCl during combustion.

## Comparison with similar materials
Compared to CPVC, the density is nearly identical (1.4 g/cm³), but CPVC raises the service temperature to 93 °C (200 °F) thanks to a higher chlorine content. Compared to PE and PP, PVC is stiffer and offers complementary chemical resistance, although with lower cold impact resistance and lower flexibility.

| Property                      | Rigid PVC (UPVC)   | CPVC                  |
|-------------------------------|---------------------|-----------------------|
| Density (g/cm³)               | 1.40                | 1.45 – 1.55           |
| Tensile strength (MPa)        | 51                  | 50 – 55               |
| Max. service temperature (°C) | 50 – 60             | 82 – 93               |
| Oxygen index (%)              | 45                  | 60                    |
| Relative cost                 | Low                 | Medium‑low            |

## Standards and specifications
- ASTM D1785: Rigid PVC pipe schedules 40, 80 and 120 for pressure water.  
- ASTM F441: CPVC pipe schedules 40 and 80 for high temperature service.  
- ASTM D2665: PVC pipe for drainage, waste and vent (DWV).  
- ASTM D2846: CPVC systems in copper tube sizes (CTS) for cold and hot water.

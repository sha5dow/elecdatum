## General information
Zinc is a bluish-white non-ferrous metal that is widely used in die casting alloys due to its excellent fluidity, high impact resistance, and ability to accept a variety of surface finishes. Zinc alloy parts are much more durable than their plastic equivalents and allow complex geometries with tight dimensional tolerances. The most common alloy used in hot-chamber die casting is designated ZP5, according to European standard EN 12844, consuming more than 60 % of the zinc cast in this industrial sector.

## Chemical composition
The chemical composition of alloy ZP5 (ZnAl4Cu1) features controlled levels of aluminum, copper, and magnesium, with strict impurity limits to ensure dimensional stability and aging resistance.

| Element | Content (%) |
|---|---|
| Zinc (Zn) | Balance |
| Aluminum (Al) | 3.7 – 4.3 |
| Copper (Cu) | 0.7 – 1.2 |
| Magnesium (Mg) | 0.025 – 0.06 |
| Iron (Fe) | 0.05 max |
| Silicon (Si) | 0.03 max |
| Nickel (Ni) | 0.02 max |
| Lead (Pb) | 0.005 max |
| Cadmium (Cd) | 0.005 max |
| Tin (Sn) | 0.002 max |

## Physical properties
ZP5 zinc alloy exhibits a relatively high density, low porosity in die casting, and good vibration damping capability.

| Property | Value (SI) | Value (Imperial) |
|---|---|---|
| Density | 6.6 – 6.7 g/cm³ | 0.238 – 0.242 lb/in³ |
| Modulus of elasticity (Young's) | 85 GPa | 12.3 × 10⁶ psi |
| Poisson's ratio | 0.29 | 0.29 |

## Mechanical properties
The mechanical properties of ZP5 alloy correspond to the as‑cast condition, which is the usual delivery state. No subsequent heat treatments are applied, so the values represent the behavior directly after solidification in a metal mold.

| Property | Value (SI) | Value (Imperial) |
|---|---|---|
| Tensile strength (Rm) | 330 MPa | 47.9 ksi |
| Yield strength (Rp0.2) | 250 MPa | 36.3 ksi |
| Elongation at break (A, L₀=50 mm) | 5 % | 5 % |
| Brinell hardness (HB) | 92 | 92 |
| Impact energy (KV, +20 °C) | 58 J | 42.8 ft·lbf |

## Thermal properties
Available thermal data for ZP5 alloy are limited to the melting range. Full thermal behavior (conductivity, specific heat) should be adapted according to the final application.

| Property | Value (SI) | Value (Imperial) |
|---|---|---|
| Melting range | 379 – 388 °C | 714 – 730 °F |

## Corrosion resistance
Zinc die casting alloys offer adequate resistance to atmospheric corrosion in indoor and moderate outdoor environments. The controlled presence of magnesium inhibits intergranular corrosion. For applications in aggressive media or prolonged unprotected outdoor exposure, a surface coating is recommended, such as nickel plating, chrome plating, electrostatic painting, or lacquering, all of which are easily applied on die‑cast zinc.

## Applications by industry
The versatility of ZP5 alloy makes it a common choice in sectors that require high-precision functional parts with aesthetic surface quality.

- **Automotive:** closure system components, mirror brackets, sensor housings, and interior parts with decorative finish.
- **Consumer electronics:** connector housings, microphone bodies, display frames, and EMI shielding elements due to its good shielding capability.
- **Hardware and construction:** door and window fittings, knobs, hinges, faucets and bathroom accessories.
- **Office equipment:** lamp bases, monitor stands, furniture accessory parts.
- **Housewares and artistic metalwork:** candelabras, handles, ornaments, and elements with aged or gold finishes.

## Comparison with similar materials
| Material | Density (g/cm³) | Tensile strength (MPa) | Yield strength (MPa) | Elongation (%) | Main advantage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zinc alloy ZP5 | 6.6 | 330 | 250 | 5 | High fluidity, low mold cost, decorative finishes |
| Aluminum alloy A380 | 2.7 | 324 | 159 | 3.5 | Lightweight, good strength at moderate temperature |
| Magnesium alloy AZ91D | 1.8 | 230 | 150 | 3 | Highest strength/weight ratio, extreme lightness |

Zinc alloys offer the advantage of casting at lower temperature, which extends mold life and allows faster cycles in hot‑chamber machines. In exchange, their higher density limits their use in components where weight is critical.

## General Overview
D2 tool steel is a high-carbon, high-chromium cold work steel, belonging to group D of the AISI classification. Its carbon content ranges from 1.40 to 1.60%, with chromium from 11.00 to 13.00%, giving it exceptional wear resistance. It exhibits air hardening and good dimensional stability, being the most widely used grade in its group for applications requiring long production runs.

## Heat treatment
The hardening process for D2 steel is carried out with a slow preheating to 815 °C (1500 °F), followed by a temperature increase to 1010 °C (1850 °F). The material is held at this austenitizing temperature for 20 to 45 minutes and then air cooled (air hardening).

## Forging
Forging of D2 steel should be carried out in a temperature range starting at 1065 °C (1950 °F) and decreasing to a minimum of 954 °C (1750 °F). Forging below 926 °C (1700 °F) is not recommended to avoid cracking.

## Annealing
Annealing is performed by heating the steel to a temperature between 871 and 898 °C (1600 – 1650 °F). Subsequently, slow furnace cooling at a rate of 4.4 °C (40 °F) per hour or less is carried out to achieve adequate softening.

## Tempering
The tempering temperature for D2 steel is typically selected between 150 °C and 540 °C, depending on the desired balance between hardness and toughness. To maintain high surface hardness (above 60 HRC), tempering in the low range is usually applied, while the high range may cause slight softening but improves toughness.

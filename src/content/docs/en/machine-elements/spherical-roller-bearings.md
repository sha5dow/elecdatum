## Types and configurations
Spherical roller bearings are self-aligning units with two rows of barrel-shaped rollers, mounted between an inner ring with two inclined raceways and an outer ring with a single spherical raceway. Their defining characteristic is the ability to accommodate angular misalignment between the shaft and housing, maintaining low friction even under demanding loads.

Configurations include cylindrical bore and tapered bore (taper 1:12 or 1:30), the latter facilitating mounting on adapter sleeves. Internal designs vary by manufacturer and include steel sheet cages, machined brass cages, or polyamide cages, as well as variants with and without a central flange. Some executions incorporate integral seals for contaminated conditions.

Series 238, 239, 240, 241 and 248/249 are distinguished by their reduced radial cross-section, ideal for confined spaces. Single-row bearings, also called barrel bearings (series 202/203), only support radial loads and practically no axial load.

## Standardized dimensions
The main dimensions —bore diameter (d), outside diameter (D) and width (B)— are standardized according to ISO 15:1998. For tapered bores, the nominal taper is 1:12 for most series and 1:30 for series 240, 241, 248 and 249.

## Load capacities
They support very high radial loads and axial loads in both directions, thanks to the symmetrical configuration of the two roller rows and the contact angle on the inner raceways. The axial load capacity depends on the contact angle and the number of rollers, typically 25 % to 50 % of the dynamic radial load rating.

## Materials and heat treatments
The rings and rollers are predominantly made of high-hardenability chrome steel, equivalent to AISI 52100 (100Cr6, SUJ2, GCr15), with approximately 1.5 % chromium and a surface hardness of 60 to 64 HRC. For corrosive or high-temperature environments, martensitic stainless steels (X65Cr13) and high-temperature steels (M50) are available, as well as surface coatings (phosphating, zinc plating, DLC coating).

Cages are selected based on size, speed and service conditions:

| Cage material | Process | Typical applications |
|-------------------|---------|----------------------|
| Steel sheet | Stamped | Light and medium series, general purpose |
| Brass | Machined or stamped | High loads, vibrations, high temperatures |
| Glass-fiber reinforced polyamide 66 | Injection molded | Medium speeds, temperatures up to 120 °C / 248 °F |
| Machined steel | Machined | Large dimensions, heavy series, shocks |

Sealed bearings incorporate nitrile rubber (NBR) or polyacrylate (ACM) and can operate in a temperature range from -40 °C to +120 °C / -40 °F to 248 °F.

## Typical applications
They are used in machines where heavy loads, moderate speeds and possible misalignments converge. The following table shows a selection guide by application.

| Application | Recommended series | Determining characteristic | Concrete example |
|------------|-------------------|-----------------------------|------------------|

| Fans and blowers | 222, 230 | Low noise level, long grease life | Induced draft centrifugal fan |

## Selection criteria
To select the appropriate bearing, it is necessary to evaluate the radial load, axial load, rotational speed, expected degree of misalignment and environmental conditions. The size is determined by calculating the equivalent dynamic load (P) and the required service life (L₁₀h) according to ISO 281.

The simplified life equation: L₁₀h = (10⁶ / 60 n) × (C / P)^(10/3), where C is the basic dynamic load rating (kN), P is the equivalent dynamic load (kN) and n is the speed (rpm). The life exponent of 10/3 reflects the typical behavior of roller bearings.

In contaminated or poorly lubricated environments, the SKF adjusted rating life method is applied, introducing the factor a_skf. It must be verified that the equivalent static load does not exceed C₀ divided by a safety factor (normally ≥2 for impact loads).

The permissible misalignment varies by series and load:

| Series | Maximum permissible misalignment under light load (degrees) |
|-------|-----------------------------------------------------------|
| 213, 222 | 1.5 |
| 223 | 2.0 |
| 230 | 2.5 |
| 231 | 1.5 |
| 232 | 2.0 |
| 240, 241 | 2.0 |
| 238, 239 | 2.0 |
| 248, 249 | 1.5 |

For applications where misalignment exceeds 0.5°, it is essential to verify that the cage does not rub against the rollers and that the seal (if present) withstands the movement.

## Mounting and fits
Mounting can be done by hot mounting, hydraulic press, or, in the case of tapered bore, using an adapter sleeve or a withdrawal sleeve. Large bearings are often cold mounted with liquid nitrogen or by induction heating.

The recommended fits according to rotation and load are described below.

| Service condition | Fit of inner ring on shaft (metric / imperial) | Fit of outer ring in housing (metric / imperial) |
|-----------------------|-------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------|
| Point load (rotating ring) | k5 to m5 (light to medium interference) | Sliding (H7 or J7) |
| Circumferential load (stationary ring) | Heavy interference (n6, p6) | Light interference (K7) when ring rotates |
| Impact or vibration loads | m6 to p6 (medium interference) | K7 with controlled interference |
| Bearings with tapered sleeve | Sleeve pushed 1.5 to 2 mm per inch of shaft diameter | Loose fit in housing (H8) |

During mounting, ensure that the bearing has adequate internal radial clearance, typically C3 or C4 for applications with heavy interference or thermal expansion. The initial recommended lubrication is with lithium soap grease of NLGI 2 consistency, filling between 30 % and 50 % of the free volume of the bearing.

## Advantages and limitations
The main advantages are self-alignment, high radial and axial load capacity, suitability for regimes with impact loads, and the possibility of mounting on tapered sleeves. As limitations, they have higher friction than rigid ball bearings and a lower limiting speed. Internal friction and heat generation increase significantly when the permitted misalignment is exceeded, reducing service life.

## Frequently asked questions
1. The maximum misalignment in a series 222 bearing is 1.5° under light load, and for series 230 it can reach 2.5°. Can permanent misalignments be compensated?  
Yes, as long as the angular misalignment does not exceed the admissible value for the series and the load does not exceed 30 % of the dynamic capacity. Higher misalignment accelerates wear and can damage the cage.

2. A bearing 22320 has a dynamic load rating of 710 kN / 159 600 lbf and a static load rating of 880 kN / 197 800 lbf. What does this mean in practice?  
The basic dynamic load rating C is the load at which 90 % of a sample of identical bearings can achieve one million revolutions without fatigue. The static load rating C₀ indicates the limit beyond which permanent deformations occur at the contacts.

3. The limiting speed with grease for a bearing 22216 is 4300 rpm. Can it be operated above if oil is used?  
Yes, with oil or oil mist lubrication the limiting speed can be increased by 20 % to 30 %, provided the lubrication system guarantees cooling and heat dissipation.

4. A spherical roller bearing of series 232 has a more slender radial cross-section than the corresponding series 223. When is series 232 preferred?  
When the available housing height space is limited but high load capacity is required, such as in compact gearboxes or large-diameter conveyor rollers.

5. The maximum temperature for a polyamide cage is 120 °C / 248 °F. Can a bearing with this cage work at 130 °C?  
No, not continuously, because the polymer loses mechanical strength and ages prematurely. For higher temperatures, a brass or steel cage should be chosen, depending on speed and vibration conditions.

6. The standardized radial clearance for a bearing with bore diameter 100 mm is 0.035 mm to 0.060 mm / 0.0014 in to 0.0024 in. When is C3 or C4 clearance required?  
When mounted with heavy interference on the shaft, when the housing and shaft have different thermal expansion, or when impact loads are dominant. C4 clearance adds about 0.030 mm / 0.0012 in of additional slack over C3.

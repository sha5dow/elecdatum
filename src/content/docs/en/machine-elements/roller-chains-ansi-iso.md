## General description
ANSI and ISO roller chains are standardized power transmission elements, composed of alternating inner links (with bushings and rollers) and outer links (with pins), which mesh with sprockets. They are mostly manufactured from carbon steel, with versions in 304 stainless steel and corrosion-resistant coated steel for aggressive environments. Continuous lubrication is critical for their service life.

## Selection criteria
The transmittable power is determined from the pitch, speed of the faster sprocket, and desired life. The nominal power method per the standard supplement is used, affected by service factors (f1), small sprocket tooth factor (f2), and multiple-strand factor (f3). The iterative selection consists of:

1. Calculate the corrected power Pc = Pmotor × f1.
2. Tentatively choose a chain pitch.
3. Verify that Pc ≤ Pnominal × f2 × f3 for the rotational speed.
4. Check the static breaking load with a safety factor ≥ 7 in applications with extreme loads.

## Installation considerations
- Ideal center distance: 30 to 50 pitches; minimum 2 to 3 times the diameter of the large sprocket.
- The slack span should work on the lower side to avoid interference.
- Angular alignment ≤ 1/4°; axial misalignment ≤ 0,2 mm / 0.008 in per 100 mm / 3.94 in of center distance.
- Correct tension: sag ≈ 2% of center distance under moderate manual load.
- Connecting links (clip type or spring pin) must be installed with the clip opening opposite to the chain travel direction.
